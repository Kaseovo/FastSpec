1. WHAT ARE COOKIES?

Cookies are small data files placed on your computer or mobile device when you visit a website. Website owners use cookies to make websites work efficiently and to provide reporting information.

Cookies set by us are called first-party cookies. Cookies set by other parties are called third-party cookies. Third-party cookies enable features such as analytics, embedded content, and advertising services.

2. WHY DO WE USE COOKIES?

We use first-party and third-party cookies for several reasons:

  • Strictly necessary cookies: Required for core website operation and security.
  • Performance and analytics cookies: Help us understand usage and improve reliability.
  • Functionality cookies: Remember preferences and improve user experience.
  • Advertising cookies: May be used by third parties to provide relevant ads and measurement.

3. HOW CAN I CONTROL COOKIES?

You can decide whether to accept or reject cookies through our cookie banner or preference controls, where available. Essential cookies cannot be rejected because they are necessary to provide the Website.

If you reject non-essential cookies, you can still use the Website, but some features may be limited.

5. WHAT ABOUT OTHER TRACKING TECHNOLOGIES?

We may use related technologies such as web beacons (also called tracking pixels or clear GIFs). These help us understand traffic patterns, improve performance, and measure communications. Many of these technologies rely on cookies.

6. DO YOU USE FLASH COOKIES OR LOCAL SHARED OBJECTS?

Some websites use Flash Cookies (Local Shared Objects, or LSOs). If used, they can store certain preferences and anti-fraud signals. You can manage Flash storage settings via Adobe tools:

  • Website Storage Settings Panel
  • Global Storage Settings Panel

Restricting Flash Cookies may affect functionality for services that rely on them.

7. DO YOU SERVE TARGETED ADVERTISING?

Third parties may place cookies to deliver and measure advertising relevant to your interests. These technologies may collect information about visits across websites. This process typically does not directly identify you by name unless you provide identifying details separately.
